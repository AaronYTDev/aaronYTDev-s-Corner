FYAISA — the one-stop homebrew shop for Vega OS (Fire TV) devices

Last edited October 11th, 2026

Wrote by aaronYTDev, edited & maintained by aaronYTDev

Say hello to FYAISA — pronounced "Fie-eye-sha," and it stands for "F**k You Amazon, I'm Sideloading Anyways." Yeah, it's that kind of project. It's the one-stop homebrew shop for Vega OS devices, and I built it with Madrinth.

Here's the problem. Amazon blocks APK sideloading on the newer sticks (Fire TV Stick 4K Select, Fire TV Stick HD, Fire TV Stick 4K) and offers no app store for sideloaded code. But Vega OS does have a real SDK and an official Developer Mode, so we just used that. Instead of fighting the launcher, FYAISA speaks the platform's own language: a catalog of Vega homebrew you can build and install on your own Fire TV.

It comes in two pieces. On your computer there's the fyaisa CLI — list, search, info, install, update, uninstall, devices, connect and approve. On your TV there's the FYAISA app, an on-device catalog browser with search and details. Everything they have in common lives in one catalog.json that every app registers itself in.

The tricky part: a Vega OS app can't install other packages for you. Package management is host-side only, and there's no third-party install API. So the builds happen on your machine — your PC fetches the source, builds it with the Vega SDK, and runs vega device install-app. That would normally mean getting off the couch every time, which is no fun. So fyaisa connect runs a small bridge on your LAN: the TV app pairs with a 6-digit code, and suddenly every app's detail screen grows an Install button. Browse on the couch, install with the remote. Pairing sticks across reboots, so you only do it once per PC.

There's even a Power tools panel for poking at the stick: launch hidden system apps the launcher won't show, see and stop running apps, run allowlisted vega device run-cmd commands from a D-pad terminal, read device info, and reboot the TV. It all goes through the same allowlisted, token-authenticated bridge — which binds to localhost unless you pass --lan, and won't accept a single request until you approve that pairing code.

And there's already an app lineup: VegaTube (ad-free YouTube, the reference app), ElevSH Files (a D-pad file browser), Google Snake, DOOM shareware compiled to WebAssembly, and Super Mario Bros via a NES emulator. Adding your own is the point — copy the reference app, tweak the manifest, register it in the catalog, and open a PR.

That's FYAISA. It's the new latest project on the front page, so go grab it if you've got a locked-down Fire TV and a bit of stubbornness. It's not affiliated with Amazon or Google — it just uses Amazon's own developer tools. Have fun, and stay off the couch.
